# Brian De Palma

## Identity
- Chinese name: 布莱恩·德·帕尔玛
- Region: United States
- Representative works: *Carrie*, *Blow Out*, *Carlito's Way*
- Best suited for: surveillance, divided attention, operatic suspense, guilt and spectacle.

## Core Dramatic Logic
Choose a situation where two dangerous actions unfold at once. Make looking itself unstable through divided screens, foreground traps, extended camera paths, or conflicting points of view.

## Light and Contrast Fingerprint
- Source hierarchy: use stage, street, apartment, public-building, practical night, and event lighting with heightened but motivated direction.
- Shadow and contrast: permit sharper theatrical contrast and color separation when surveillance or performance spaces justify it.
- Highlights and visibility: reflective floors, glass, instruments, lenses, and faces may receive graphic highlights that connect watcher and event.
- Reject: unmotivated nightclub glow, generic noir, random spotlights, or darkness that erases simultaneous action.

## Color and Exposure Fingerprint
- Palette and saturation: allow bolder reds, blues, ambers, skin, costume, and practical-event color organized around danger and performance.
- Skin and midtones: keep faces readable across divided zones even when source colors differ.
- Blacks, whites, exposure: use rich blacks, bright practicals, and controlled saturation without comic-book clipping.
- Reject: arbitrary neon, teal-orange action grade, vintage wash, or color disconnected from spectacle and guilt.

## Lens and Camera Fingerprint
- Distance and lens: combine wide spatial setup, long-lens surveillance, split focus, or split-screen-like simultaneous depth when physically coherent.
- Height and position: use balconies, stairs, corridors, streets, stages, windows, or recording positions that expose multiple routes.
- Movement and focus: employ extended tracks, circular moves, crane-like reveals, or exact focus division only when they synchronize threats.
- Witness role: a flamboyant but spatially accountable voyeur, not a generic action camera; reject technique without simultaneous information.

## Composition and Spatial Fingerprint
- Visual center: divide attention between watcher and event, rescue and threat, or foreground clue and background action.
- Layers and obstruction: use rails, glass, stages, doorways, crowds, screens, and recording devices.
- Blocking and geometry: choreograph converging routes and delayed recognition across multiple planes.
- Information control: show viewers two pieces of danger that characters cannot combine in time.

## Blocking and Story Translation
- Moment: choose simultaneous actions approaching collision.
- Character action: watch, record, cross, signal, pursue, or miss.
- Visual center: split attention between at least two causal planes.
- Blocking: synchronize routes with a visible deadline.
- Environment: make stairs, stage, crowd, or recording position structure the collision.

## Capture Texture
Use controlled optical sharpness, practical flare, split focus, and physically legible camera movement. Avoid empty technical showmanship and synthetic spectacle.

## Common Misreadings
- Using split screen without simultaneous causality.
- Copying Hitchcock shots.
- Adding saturated color without performance context.
- Making long camera moves spatially impossible.
- Turning suspense into generic action coverage.

## Nearest-Neighbor Contrast
Unlike Alfred Hitchcock, De Palma displays divided attention more overtly: higher-contrast event light, bolder practical color, elaborate roaming or split-focus camera, and simultaneous planes competing in one design rather than economical source light, disciplined color, concise point-of-view shifts, and a single knowledge mismatch.

## Model-Facing Style Anchor
Use motivated theatrical contrast, bolder practical color, spatially accountable extended camera movement, divided focus or simultaneous planes, and two causal actions converging while different characters hold incomplete knowledge.

## Default Iconic Anchor
Reselect the scene as two visible actions nearing collision; position the camera where both routes can coexist, use one controlled extended move or split-depth relation to transfer attention, and heighten practical light without losing geography.

## Scene Translation
- Scene: a sound technician sees a cyclist approach a blocked tunnel while recording from a bridge.
- Translation: a long lateral move keeps the recorder and waveform foregrounded as the cyclist and an unmarked car converge below under sodium lamps and red maintenance lights.
