# Alfred Hitchcock

## Identity
- Chinese name: 阿尔弗雷德·希区柯克
- Region: United Kingdom / United States
- Representative works: *Rear Window*, *Vertigo*, *Psycho*
- Best suited for: suspense, restricted knowledge, voyeurism, guilt, spatial traps.

## Core Dramatic Logic
Choose the moment a character knows too little or sees too much. Suspense comes from a precise mismatch between viewer knowledge, character knowledge, and an actionable spatial route.

## Light and Contrast Fingerprint
- Source hierarchy: use windows, lamps, corridor practicals, street light, bathroom fixtures, and daylight with clear narrative direction.
- Shadow and contrast: shape readable zones of observation and concealment; contrast clarifies who can see whom.
- Highlights and visibility: a key object, doorway, face turn, or reflective surface may receive controlled emphasis without becoming an effects cue.
- Reject: random noir shadow, jump-scare darkness, colored danger light, or fog that obscures suspense geography.

## Color and Exposure Fingerprint
- Palette and saturation: use controlled costume, décor, skin, and source color to guide attention or psychological association.
- Skin and midtones: keep faces readable enough for recognition and withheld reaction.
- Blacks, whites, exposure: maintain clean narrative midtones, protected whites, and strategic blacks at concealed routes.
- Reject: blanket vintage grading, red danger coding everywhere, teal-orange thriller color, or desaturation that hides clues.

## Lens and Camera Fingerprint
- Distance and lens: use objective wides for geography, subjective point-of-view relations, and measured closer lenses for recognition or mistaken identity.
- Height and position: locate the camera at the character's actual window, doorway, stair, seat, or approach route.
- Movement and focus: pan, track, dolly, or transfer focus to change knowledge; every move must alter suspense information.
- Witness role: a controlled accomplice to looking, not a flamboyant omniscient camera; reject virtuosity without point-of-view consequence.

## Composition and Spatial Fingerprint
- Visual center: bind watcher, observed zone, and threat route.
- Layers and obstruction: use windows, stairs, mirrors, doors, curtains, railings, and foreground bodies as knowledge barriers.
- Blocking and geometry: stage approach, concealment, mistaken sight lines, and delayed crossing with exact timing.
- Information control: decide precisely what viewers know before or after the character.

## Blocking and Story Translation
- Moment: choose discovery before confirmation or danger approaching an unaware subject.
- Character action: watch, hide, cross, reach, hesitate, or misrecognize.
- Visual center: privilege the sight line and its blind spot.
- Blocking: keep watcher, target, and route spatially legible.
- Environment: make architecture control access and knowledge.

## Capture Texture
Use clean narrative optics, controlled exposure, readable faces and objects, and precise spatial continuity. Avoid generic vintage polish or horror grime.

## Common Misreadings
- Treating any suspense scene as Hitchcockian.
- Copying famous shots or set pieces.
- Using a dolly effect without subjective reason.
- Hiding geography in darkness.
- Making color motifs obvious and symbolic.

## Nearest-Neighbor Contrast
Unlike Brian De Palma, Hitchcock keeps point of view classically economical: cleaner source light, controlled but less flamboyant color, camera moves tied to a single knowledge change, and concise watcher-route geometry rather than saturated theatrical contrast, split-screen or split-focus display, elaborate roaming camera, and multiple simultaneous surveillance planes.

## Model-Facing Style Anchor
Use precise point-of-view control, readable source light, disciplined color, physically located camera, watcher/observed/threat geometry, and one knowledge mismatch that turns architecture into suspense.

## Default Iconic Anchor
Reselect the beat when seeing creates danger but before confirmation; place the camera at a character's actual viewing position, map the blind route clearly, use controlled source light and selective emphasis, and make one movement or cut change who knows what.

## Scene Translation
- Scene: a hotel clerk realizes a guest has entered the wrong room.
- Translation: from the clerk's fixed desk sight line, the guest disappears behind a stair landing while a key tag remains visible; one restrained pan reveals the unlit service corridor the guest cannot see.
