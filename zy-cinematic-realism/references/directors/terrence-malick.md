# Terrence Malick

## Identity
- Chinese name: 泰伦斯·马力克
- Region: United States
- Representative works: *Days of Heaven*, *The Thin Red Line*, *The Tree of Life*
- Best suited for: natural light, tactile attention, fragmented memory.

## Core Logic
Let lived sensation lead: grass underfoot, wind against fabric, a hand on worn wood, changing light across a face. Keep it connected to a real action.

## Story and Character
Choose an interval between decisions, especially outdoors or near a threshold. Characters can move, touch, or notice rather than pose.

## Camera and Space
Use a close, drifting or observational position only where a human camera could travel. Balance intimate detail with surrounding life, air, and material movement.

## Light and Color
Favor real sun, cloud, dusk, window light, and reflected surfaces, but natural light does not always mean beautiful light. Allow faces to pass through uneven exposure and let practical darkness leave parts of an ordinary room unresolved. Do not invent golden-hour light at the wrong time.

## Best Use Cases
Rural work, childhood memory, family passage, landscape and private reflection.

## Common Misreadings
- Perpetual sun flare.
- Abstract whispery montage.
- Backlit fashion portrait.
- Random nature inserts.
- Golden-hour beauty used in place of lived sensation.
- Turning curtains, windows, or skin highlights into perfume-ad imagery.
- A polished wide composition that keeps the whole room attractively visible.

## Narrative Priority Override

In `strong` or `iconic` mode, do not center procedural information relationships. A clue, document, or projected face may remain present, but it should not automatically become the clean geometric center of the image.

Prefer the bodily moment immediately before or after the procedural action:

- the card lowering after recognition;
- a hand losing its exact position against the wall;
- a sleeve catching a curled paper edge;
- the detective stepping through the projector beam;
- the projected face breaking across a shoulder, wall texture, and incomplete papers;
- fatigue briefly interrupting the comparison process.

The investigation must remain understandable, but emotional and bodily duration should outrank perfect evidence legibility.

## Signature Overrides

When strength is `strong` or `iconic`, enforce the following differences:

- Story beat bias: choose a sensory interval between decisions rather than the decision explained aloud.
- Blocking pattern: let bodies pass through air, cloth, doorways, and light rather than arrange them as a diagram.
- Camera height: stay near body, hand, or waist height when the location permits it.
- Camera distance / lens behavior: allow intimate, unstable proximity with partial bodies and interrupted gestures; do not make the whole room equally poetic or complete.
- Movement grammar: use a short, human-paced drift, turn, or follow movement motivated by a person or moving air; the camera may briefly lose perfect framing or complete object readability.
- Light and contrast: favor real dusk, window, cloud, and reflected light without treating it as beauty light; allow uneven facial exposure and practical darkness to interrupt visibility.
- Color behavior: use material color and changing light, not a golden wash.
- Depth-of-field strategy: let focus breathe or soften for physically motivated movement; do not turn every frame into synthetic bokeh.
- Texture / capture character: retain skin, fabric, paper, paint, and air as varied tactile surfaces without smoothing or halo effects; one bodily or tactile detail should dominate rather than making every surface lyrical.
- Environment participation: wind, curtains, paper, temperature, floor texture, and room air must influence gestures and rhythm without becoming decorative atmosphere.

## Camera Signature Lock

For `iconic`:

- use a nearby, physically possible 24-28mm spatial feeling rather than a clean compressed evidence-detail composition;
- keep the camera within bodily proximity, not simply as a telephoto crop;
- allow a real one-step drift, imperfect lateral movement, or momentary loss of alignment;
- crop part of the face, shoulder, hand, or evidence naturally;
- do not align the employee card and projected face as a perfect graphic comparison;
- allow the card or projected face to pass briefly outside ideal focus;
- avoid elegant centered projection compositions;
- avoid stable prestige-crime coverage.

## Light and Color Signature Lock

For `iconic`:

- do not reuse the baseline's clean low-key grey-green crime-drama palette by default;
- allow mixed, imperfect fluorescent contamination and projector white;
- allow parts of skin to become dull, slightly underexposed, or briefly washed by projection;
- permit clipped or unresolved portions of the projection;
- do not build a beautiful spotlight around the clue;
- do not add a polished vignette;
- preserve unattractive neutral paper, tired skin, dead metal, and uneven wall color.

## Focus and Texture Signature Lock

- focus may breathe between hand, paper edge, sleeve, and projected wall;
- only one tactile event should dominate at a time;
- do not keep the card, projected face, timestamp, wall evidence, and detective equally legible;
- grain and softness must feel captured, not applied as a prestige-film finish.

## Model-Facing Style Anchor

- Explicit director token: Terrence Malick
- Representative film anchors: *Days of Heaven*, *The Thin Red Line*, *The Tree of Life*
- Strong visual grammar: nearby physically possible 24-28mm drifting camera; partial bodies and interrupted gestures moving through air, cloth, skin, and paper; one tactile event dominates; uneven available-light exposure and practical darkness; material color rather than a golden wash; breathing focus; captured softness and varied surface texture; emotion carried by bodily duration and environment rather than procedural information.
- Do not reduce to: golden backlight, wheat fields, sky-gazing, touching plants, perfume-ad beauty, or a pastoral metaphor.
- Prompt insertion: Director reference: Terrence Malick, with broad visual reference points in *Days of Heaven*, *The Thin Red Line*, and *The Tree of Life*. Use bodily proximity, a physically possible drifting wide-angle camera, tactile interruptions, fragmentary duration, uneven available-light exposure, partial bodies, breathing focus, environmental movement, and emotion carried by air, cloth, skin, paper, and gesture rather than procedural information.

## Default Iconic Anchor

Director and visual reference: Terrence Malick, drawing strongly from the visual language associated with Days of Heaven, The Thin Red Line, and The Tree of Life. Let bodily duration and tactile interruption outrank procedural evidence clarity: a nearby physically possible wide-angle camera drifts one human step behind the character, a hand loses its perfect task position, and a paper edge, cloth, or moving air interrupts the action. Use breathing focus, partial bodies, unstable available-light exposure, mixed dead practical contamination, dull skin, matte paper, unresolved corners, and captured rather than polished texture; do not reduce the scene to golden backlight, fields, sky-gazing, or perfume-ad beauty.

## Scene Translation
- story beat: after comparing an employee card with the projected surveillance face, a detective has just lowered the card as fatigue interrupts the next check.
- dominant tactile event: a curled typed-paper edge brushes his sleeve while the lowered card stays visible near the frame edge.
- camera position: nearby at shoulder height, one step behind and to the side of the detective, with his shoulder between the lens and the evidence wall.
- camera movement: a real one-step lateral drift that briefly loses the card-to-projection alignment.
- focus behavior: focus breathes from the sleeve and paper edge toward the broken projection; the card and projected face are not simultaneously ideal.
- light behavior: imperfect fluorescent contamination mixes with projector white, leaving dull skin, dead metal, uneven wall color, and unresolved projection portions rather than a beautiful clue spotlight.
- fixed facts kept peripheral or incomplete: the employee card remains near the frame edge, the projected face breaks across the detective's shoulder and uneven paper, and maps, photographs, tape, coffee, file boxes, and cabinets stay present but partly obscured or soft.
- avoid: perfect card-to-face alignment, centered projection geometry, stable prestige-crime coverage, grey-green low-key default, beautiful projector beam, polished vignette, perfume-ad light, complete evidence-room tableau.
