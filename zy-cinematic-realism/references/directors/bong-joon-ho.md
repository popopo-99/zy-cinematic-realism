# Bong Joon-ho

## Identity
- Chinese name: 奉俊昊
- Region: South Korea
- Representative works: *Memories of Murder*, *Parasite*, *Mother*
- Best suited for: class systems, procedural failure, dark comedy, sudden tonal violence.

## Core Dramatic Logic
Choose a practical task whose social hierarchy becomes visible through space. Comedy, procedure, embarrassment, and danger can coexist until one blocking change reverses control.

## Light and Contrast Fingerprint
- Source hierarchy: use daylight, basement windows, fluorescents, domestic fixtures, rain, vehicle lights, and institutional practicals.
- Shadow and contrast: make class or access differences visible through bright and dark levels, thresholds, and weather.
- Highlights and visibility: keep crucial props and reactions readable before a tonal reversal sends part of the frame into harder contrast.
- Reject: moody darkness without social geography, elegant thriller keys, decorative rain, or comedic flatness everywhere.

## Color and Exposure Fingerprint
- Palette and saturation: use believable domestic, institutional, street, and weather color with strategic contrasts between social zones.
- Skin and midtones: keep faces readable enough for group reaction while allowing harsh source shifts during reversal.
- Blacks, whites, exposure: hold accessible midtones, then use deeper blacks or clipped weather highlights at the turn.
- Reject: uniform green class coding, teal-orange thriller grade, luxury color polish, or cartoon saturation.

## Lens and Camera Fingerprint
- Distance and lens: favor wide or normal group views that reveal levels, routes, and reaction chains; use closer frames at reversal.
- Height and position: place the camera on stairs, corridors, rooms, streets, or floor levels where social access is legible.
- Movement and focus: track or reframe precisely with blocking; maintain deep enough focus for setup and background consequence.
- Witness role: a socially alert storyteller, not a neutral institution or flamboyant stylist; reject incoherent handheld chaos.

## Composition and Spatial Fingerprint
- Visual center: connect a practical task to a class boundary, hidden route, or reaction chain.
- Layers and obstruction: use stairs, windows, doors, tables, basements, roads, and weather as social structure.
- Blocking and geometry: arrange ensembles so status changes can be seen through height, access, and movement.
- Information control: reveal more to viewers than to at least one character, enabling dread or dark comedy.

## Blocking and Story Translation
- Moment: choose a routine task one beat before social control reverses.
- Character action: carry, serve, search, hide, clean, or wait while another group enters.
- Visual center: privilege the route or boundary where status changes.
- Blocking: keep a chain of reactions across levels.
- Environment: make stairs, rain, doors, or service spaces redistribute power.

## Capture Texture
Use clear spatial capture, ordinary surfaces, weather, bodily awkwardness, and tonal flexibility. Avoid prestige gloss and caricature.

## Common Misreadings
- Treating class themes as visual symbolism only.
- Copying stairs without power logic.
- Making dark comedy cartoonish.
- Using a green filter for poverty.
- Hiding ensemble reactions in shallow focus.

## Nearest-Neighbor Contrast
Unlike Park Chan-wook, Bong prioritizes social geography and reaction chains: more functional source light, ordinary contrasting color, wide ensemble camera, and status shifts across levels rather than sensual high-contrast lighting, controlled jewel tones, expressive camera geometry, and psychologically charged object-body compositions.

## Model-Facing Style Anchor
Use readable social levels, practical source contrast, ordinary but strategically separated color, wide ensemble geography, precise reaction chains, and a mundane task that tips into danger or dark comedy through blocking.

## Default Iconic Anchor
Reselect the scene one beat before social control reverses; map the relevant levels, doorways, or stairs in a wide group frame, keep setup props and reactions readable, and let one entrance or movement reorder status without losing physical realism.

## Scene Translation
- Scene: catering staff clear a corporate banquet after executives leave.
- Translation: a service elevator opens on an executive returning for a missing phone; a wide kitchen-door view keeps wet floor, stacked dishes, staff reactions, and the raised dining room visible across three levels of practical light.
