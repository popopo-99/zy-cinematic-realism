# Asset Pack / 视觉资产包

Prepare the visual references needed to continue a scene from an available frame. Start from the subsequent shots, not a standard inventory of turnaround sheets. Reuse the Scene Master, Continuity Bible, Reference Roles, Dream Decode, model adapters, and Result Repair; do not create a second source of scene facts.

## Entry and Scope

Use when the user requests asset preparation or asks which references are needed for subsequent shots. A normal image edit, a series, or a request for one prompt does not automatically require an asset pack. Infer the deliverable from the request; do not print a mode menu or a compulsory form.

Planning, prompts, actual reference images, and integration into later shots are different deliverables. Execute only the requested stages. A clearly authorized end-to-end visual prototype may continue through derivation, shot use, inspection, and repair without asking permission at every stage. A request to review first retains that review boundary. No paid video generation, external publication, or automatic storage follows from asset preparation.

## Recover the Actual Starting State

Inspect accessible source images before claiming visual facts. Reuse established story, model/frontend, ratio, references, and restrictions. Distinguish:

- explicit user locks and accepted proposals;
- visible source facts, including current hand/prop state;
- hidden or unavailable information;
- new design extensions proposed for the next shot.

A clear face image is not evidence of an unseen costume back, shoe, room corner, or character height. A newly generated view does not establish what was originally hidden. If exact identity preservation requires an unavailable image, request that image; a text-only inventory can proceed with the limitation stated. Never invent an attachment, reference URL, image ID, seed, or tool capability.

Low light, distance, or grain may leave a fabric pattern, hand identity, or small mark uncertain. Describe only what is supported; keep uncertainty or label a design proposal instead of calling an inferred micro-detail visible. Retain the current supported prop/hand state unless the requested next action needs a clearly stated change.

Record acceptance by scope: look, identity, wardrobe, composition, or another explicitly supported dimension. Approval of a look direction does not approve every derived asset or certify geometry. Execution authorization is not content approval. An explicitly authorized prototype may use provisional candidates while keeping that status. Preserve user-rejected outputs as failure records when useful; do not silently reintroduce them as the accepted look.

## Derive the Smallest Needed Set

Read the requested next actions and viewpoints, then identify missing references:

- **Character / wardrobe:** only views or details needed to identify the character or resolve the requested costume. A full body, back view, or turnaround is optional.
- **Prop:** construction, distinctive features, size relationships, ownership and hand use, orientation, open/closed state, contents and marks that matter to the shot.
- **Location:** visible materials and topology, an empty plate, or a necessary additional view. Removal fills and newly revealed space remain inferred or proposed; an empty plate is not a measured 3D reconstruction.
- **Look:** an accepted frame or relevant Decode rules, kept separate from asset structure. Actual style references enter Dream Decode by their assigned roles.

Reuse sufficient existing assets. Do not regenerate neutral character or object references merely because the final look changes. Do not require a fixed image count, full multi-view inventory, specific medium, dark tone, New York setting, or 2.33:1 ratio.

For each necessary asset, keep only the information needed to use it: actual source, role and exclusions, observed versus proposed content, status, dependent shots, and the most consequential check. IDs/revisions and a visible manifest are useful for a package or handoff, not mandatory for a simple request. A compact text record is sufficient; this is not a database or a claim of automatic persistence.

## Establish the Visual Direction Before Expansion

If the user needs art direction or has rejected the look, resolve the narrative frame and the assigned visual grammar before making further images its dependents. Use the existing story-development or Dream Decode workflow; do not default to portraits simply to make identities legible.

The purpose of an asset view determines its presentation. A casting or prop reference may use clear neutral light. That clarity, background, pose, crop, or texture emphasis is not authority over the final narrative image. For a non-photographic reference, preserve its authorized making logic through the existing compiler.

Compile each requested shot from:

`Scene Master + current story/prop state + relevant accepted visual grammar + role-scoped assets → selected model adapter`

Honor explicit user locks first. An identity-only reference controls identity; a prop reference controls the object. Neither may overwrite the user's look, medium, camera choice, or local light without that authority. Reference-sheet backgrounds and panel layouts must not leak into the scene.

## Generate and Inspect Only When Requested

Use real available generation tools and follow their image-handling requirements. Prompt completion is not generation. Persist project-bound images when files are requested, preserving original outputs and exact prompts or enough input-role provenance to reproduce the attempt. Report the tool/model information actually returned; do not claim a product prompt pins an underlying API model.

New unseen content is a candidate design until accepted. Do not call a generated asset validated merely because it exists or resembles the prompt. On subsequent edits, retain cumulative accepted changes and change only the authorized scope.

## Inspect Shots by Purpose

For narrative frames, first check the current action, gaze/relationship, physical viewing position, usable space, relevant visual grammar and information hierarchy. Then check the assets in the dimensions they own. Portraits, prop studies, and location plates have their own requested purpose; do not force narrative obscurity onto identification views.

Check the highest-risk continuity facts:

- identity/wardrobe outside allowed changes;
- ownership, hand occupancy, object count and contents;
- open/closed and before/after states;
- marks and orientation on the physical object, distinguishing a valid camera/object rotation from a mirrored or relocated feature;
- topology and actual light sources within the evidence available;
- reference-role leakage and loss of accepted medium/look.

A planned state is not proof it appeared. If a box remains open when half closure was requested, record that difference and repair the state when consequential. Do not certify an exact angle from a single frame, or unseen contents after an object enters a pocket. A time jump, new source light, or requested closer view may be a valid adaptation; do not repair every change back into the master.

## Repair and Revision Dependencies

Choose up to three evidence-backed dominant failures using Result Repair. Preserve successful facts. A prop-state or mark failure may need a local edit; a failed story action, witness position, or spatial structure may need rebuilding from the Scene Master. A rejected look should be corrected at the visual-grammar/compilation step, not covered by further object-detail edits.

Track only affected dependencies. A changed look may require shot or location-plate review without invalidating identity structure. A new wardrobe or prop ownership may affect later frames even if their scene numbers differ. Exclude rejected results from the accepted state. Keep generation, inspection, user acceptance and unresolved issues separate in handoff.

## Output

- **Plan only:** necessary assets, missing/proposed information, and how they will be used.
- **Prompts:** requested asset prompts and concise role/shot instructions. Prompt-only returns only the requested prompts; use the existing model gate, without a new questionnaire.
- **Actual visual prototype:** requested images, useful role mapping, subsequent shots when requested, actual inspection/repair findings, and file/provenance records when delivering a package.
- **Handoff:** use Project Handoff with relevant asset roles, accepted scopes, availability, current states, and next task. A filename or card title cannot recover an unavailable image.

Do not append the entire manifest, a standard acceptance checklist, or an asset pack to ordinary image output. Workflow compliance, image resemblance, a single qualitative example and general quality/efficiency gains are separate claims.
