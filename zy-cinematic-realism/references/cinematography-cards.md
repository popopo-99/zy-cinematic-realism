# Original Cinematography Cards

These are camera-and-light methods, not imitations of cinematographers. Applying a card must change witness role, camera behavior, source-light behavior, or spatial grammar. Camera brands, focal-length badges, film stock, and grain alone do not count.

## Natural Source Precision / 自然光源精准控制

- **Best for:** restrained interiors, portraits inside real spaces, and mixed daylight/practical scenes.
- **Witness position:** where source direction and subject blocking are both legible.
- **Distance:** medium-wide to close, chosen by the source footprint.
- **Focal behavior:** neutral; perspective remains spatially honest.
- **Movement:** locked or minimally adjusted.
- **Depth / focus:** enough depth to connect subject, source, and receiving surfaces.
- **Source light behavior:** one primary source; secondary sources affect only their reachable zones.
- **Exposure:** protect the primary highlight while allowing natural falloff.
- **Material response:** specify which surfaces absorb, scatter, reflect, or transmit.
- **Composition tendency:** source direction organizes the visual center rather than a centered face.
- **Failure risks:** decorative fill, equal face exposure, source multiplication.
- **Do not confuse with:** “beautiful natural light” without a source map.

## Available-Light Handheld / 现有光手持观察

- **Best for:** public life, domestic tension, fieldwork, and unstable proximity.
- **Witness position:** a standing or seated human participant at the edge of action.
- **Distance:** close enough to feel present, far enough to retain spatial cause.
- **Focal behavior:** moderately open or neutral; no cosmetic distortion.
- **Movement:** restrained handheld drift or physically plausible micro-instability.
- **Depth / focus:** selective misses are allowed when movement explains them.
- **Source light behavior:** use only existing daylight, practicals, and incidental spill.
- **Exposure:** accept brief underexposure, clipped practicals, or source-driven fluctuation.
- **Material response:** skin, fabric, walls, and glass remain unevenly legible.
- **Composition tendency:** responsive framing, partial exits, and imperfect timing.
- **Failure risks:** fake documentary shake, random blur, chaotic framing.
- **Do not confuse with:** low quality, surveillance footage, or noise filters.

## Wide Environmental Witness / 环境宽景目击

- **Best for:** labor, isolation, architecture, infrastructure, and landscape duty.
- **Witness position:** a reachable fixed point that explains routes and scale.
- **Distance:** wide or extreme wide; people remain narratively readable through action.
- **Focal behavior:** spatially open without impossible expansion.
- **Movement:** locked-off or slow observational drift.
- **Depth / focus:** layered depth with foreground, working zone, and distant system.
- **Source light behavior:** broad environmental source; local practicals remain subordinate.
- **Exposure:** prioritize spatial continuity over face exposure.
- **Material response:** terrain, concrete, water, metal, and weather establish scale.
- **Composition tendency:** small subjects, route lines, and functional negative space.
- **Failure risks:** tourism panorama, drone spectacle, unreadably tiny action.
- **Do not confuse with:** “epic wide shot” or generic establishing coverage.

## Compressed Nocturnal Observation / 夜间压缩观察

- **Best for:** distant surveillance, traffic, crowds, inaccessible private gestures, and night transit.
- **Witness position:** across a street, platform, vehicle lane, or deep interior layer.
- **Distance:** long observation distance.
- **Focal behavior:** compressed depth and layered source overlap.
- **Movement:** locked, supported, or slow track from a real platform.
- **Depth / focus:** selective planes; intervening traffic or glass may interrupt clarity.
- **Source light behavior:** practical pools overlap without becoming a neon wash.
- **Exposure:** protect bright signs and lamps; accept deep inactive zones.
- **Material response:** wet surfaces, glass, paint, and metal catch sources selectively.
- **Composition tendency:** stacked spatial planes and withheld access.
- **Failure risks:** paparazzi cliché, neon noir, arbitrary bokeh, flattened topology.
- **Do not confuse with:** a portrait merely labeled “85mm at night.”

## Hard Direct Flash / 现场硬直闪

- **Best for:** sudden documentation, nightlife exits, evidence, crowded interiors, or uncomfortable immediacy.
- **Witness position:** close, frontal or oblique, at human-held camera height.
- **Distance:** close to medium; flash falloff must be visible.
- **Focal behavior:** neutral to moderately open.
- **Movement:** instant capture; ambient motion may remain behind the flash-frozen zone.
- **Depth / focus:** active plane is crisp while background falls rapidly into ambient darkness.
- **Source light behavior:** one on-axis or near-axis flash dominates; ambient sources survive where exposure permits.
- **Exposure:** hard foreground highlights, quick falloff, and limited background reach.
- **Material response:** skin, glass, reflective fabric, paint, and dust react sharply and differently.
- **Composition tendency:** abrupt cropping, crowd proximity, imperfect social distance.
- **Failure risks:** fashion campaign, uniform white blast, red-carpet polish.
- **Do not confuse with:** studio strobe shaping or soft beauty fill.

## Deep-Focus Spatial Blocking / 深焦空间调度

- **Best for:** ensembles, procedural spaces, social hierarchy, and simultaneous actions.
- **Witness position:** where foreground, middle action, and background consequence align without cheating topology.
- **Distance:** medium-wide to wide.
- **Focal behavior:** spatially open or neutral with controlled perspective.
- **Movement:** locked or motivated lateral/revealing movement.
- **Depth / focus:** multiple action planes remain intentionally legible.
- **Source light behavior:** sources separate zones without isolating only one face.
- **Exposure:** maintain readable midtones across depth while preserving real falloff.
- **Material response:** atmospheric distance and surface contrast prevent flatness.
- **Composition tendency:** eye-lines, doorways, furniture, and routes connect simultaneous events.
- **Failure risks:** stagey tableau, equal importance everywhere, impossible focus, clutter.
- **Do not confuse with:** simply adding “deep focus” to a centered group shot.

## Low-Key Practical Interior / 实景灯低照度室内

- **Best for:** late-night rooms, offices, workshops, vehicles, and private aftermath.
- **Witness position:** inside the practical light's real spill, not in an impossible black void.
- **Distance:** medium or close with surrounding darkness retained.
- **Focal behavior:** neutral or gently compressed.
- **Movement:** locked or careful handheld.
- **Depth / focus:** one or two readable zones; inactive depth may disappear.
- **Source light behavior:** lamps, fixtures, screens, or corridor spill have limited reach.
- **Exposure:** protect practical cores and skin highlights; preserve unreadable shadow.
- **Material response:** matte surfaces sink; glass, eyes, metal, and worn edges catch narrow highlights.
- **Composition tendency:** lit islands separated by functional darkness.
- **Failure risks:** crushed featureless blacks, invented rim light, glossy noir polish.
- **Do not confuse with:** underexposure alone or teal-orange night grading.

## Overcast Material Realism / 阴天材质写实

- **Best for:** streets, industrial sites, historical exteriors, wet weather, and subdued portraiture.
- **Witness position:** at ground level where surfaces and spatial depth remain observable.
- **Distance:** wide to close depending on the material relationship.
- **Focal behavior:** neutral; no need for optical spectacle.
- **Movement:** locked, slow, or restrained handheld.
- **Depth / focus:** moderate depth; aerial perspective follows moisture and distance.
- **Source light behavior:** broad sky source with weak directional cues and local occlusion.
- **Exposure:** protect pale sky and wet highlights without HDR recovery everywhere.
- **Material response:** stone, metal, fabric, skin, paint, and water separate through roughness and saturation.
- **Composition tendency:** tonal layers, muted planes, and small color anchors.
- **Failure risks:** gray mud, fake wet gloss, flat subject cutout, melancholy filter.
- **Do not confuse with:** low contrast everywhere or desaturation as style.
